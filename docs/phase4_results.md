# Phase 4 results: RQ6, requirements against public reporting

Project: *The solar boom's hidden bill: Nigeria's coming wave of solar panel and battery waste*
Run: 2026-11-05. It follows `docs/analysis_plan.md` §11 (A1-A7). No result of RQ1-RQ5 is changed.

**Method** (plan §11):

- Each operative duty in S.I. No. 11 of 2024 is coded, together with the S.I. No. 79 of 2022 duties that apply to PV panels.
- **Coding** (Amendment A7) uses four categories: *public record of implementation found*, *public statement of intent only*, *public record indicates not implemented*, *no public record found*.
  - *Public record indicates not implemented* is used **only where a source states non-implementation explicitly**.
  - A separate column, `enforcement_action` (yes/no, with source), records documented enforcement action on the duty.
- **Sources searched:** NESREA, ARBR, EPRON, the Federal Ministry of Environment, GIZ 2025 and Nigerian press from 21 March 2024, on 2026-11-05.
- **Fixed rule.** *"No public record found" is not evidence of non-compliance, and is not reported as such* (plan §9 item 11).
- **File:** `data/processed/rq6_regulation_review.csv` (17 duties). Raw pages: `data/raw/phase4/` (not tracked).

## Quotes and notes

- **D01, Reg. 11(1); Tenth Schedule.**
  - Evidence: NESREA 'says it will begin enforcement of the Extended Producers Responsibility (EPR) guidelines and regulations' (D-G, 4 Aug 2026, Kano regulatory dialogue). (EnviroNews, 'NESREA to enforce EPR regulations against environmental pollution - D-G', 2026-08-05; https://www.environewsnigeria.com/nesrea-to-enforce-epr-regulations-against-environmental-pollution-d-g/)
  - Note: ARBR is registered as the battery-sector PRO (GIZ 2025 p.20; ThisDay 2026-04-24). No published count of subscribed producers was found. Leadership article 'only 128 firms comply' returned HTTP 403 and was not read.
- **D02, Reg. 11(2).**
  - Note: A pre-commencement record exists: REA adoption of EPR with a battery recycling fee per installed battery (Boell, 10 Dec 2021). No record after 21 March 2024 was found.
- **D03, Reg. 11(3).**
  - Note: No public registration figures or register of battery producers were found. The Leadership compliance article (HTTP 403) could not be read.
- **D04, Reg. 11(4); Tenth Schedule para 3(d).**
  - Evidence: 'At the core of the programme is the deployment of specially designed collection receptacles across strategic locations in the Federal Capital Territory, including markets, schools, offices, and motor parks', implemented 'in partnership with the Alliance for Responsible Battery Recycling (ARBR)'. (ThisDay / Arise News, 'Nigeria launches nationwide drive to safely manage small battery waste', 2026-04-24; https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/04/24/nigeria-launches-nationwide-drive-to-safely-manage-small-battery-waste/)
  - Note: The record covers small batteries in the Federal Capital Territory only; no nationwide record was found.
- **D05, Reg. 11(5).**
  - Evidence: ARBR is 'the Producer Responsibility Organisation for Nigeria's battery sector' and partner in the FCT collection programme; NESREA's D-G gave a goodwill message. (ThisDay / Arise News, 'Nigeria launches nationwide drive to safely manage small battery waste', 2026-04-24; https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/04/24/nigeria-launches-nationwide-drive-to-safely-manage-small-battery-waste/)
  - Note: The record shows the PRO operating a programme. The number of persons working with the PRO is not reported.
- **D06, Reg. 11(6), (8), (9), (11).**
  - Note: A pre-commencement ARBR page (Feb 2022) states that NESREA authorised fees, without amounts. No fee schedule, amounts or collection figures after 21 March 2024 were found. No fee amounts appear in S.I. No. 11 Reg. 11 or Schedules 10-16.
- **D07, Reg. 11(7).**
- **D08, Reg. 11(10).**
  - Evidence: Regulatory dialogue 'to clearly define the roles and responsibilities of stakeholders at the federal, state and local government levels in implementing the battery control regulations'. (EnviroNews, 'NESREA to enforce EPR regulations against environmental pollution - D-G', 2026-08-05; https://www.environewsnigeria.com/nesrea-to-enforce-epr-regulations-against-environmental-pollution-d-g/)
  - Note: No published report of PRO supervision was found.
- **D09, Tenth Schedule para (2).**
  - Note: No published EPR annual reports or aggregate figures were found.
- **D10, Tenth Schedule para (8).**
  - Note: GIZ 2025 lists ten ULAB plants and maps those in Ogun State (Figure 2-3, p.12; Table 2-1, p.11) but does not give locations by zone (plants anonymised). No public record of facilities by zone was found.
- **D11, Reg. 48(1)(j).**
  - Evidence: 'Currently, no such testing is conducted in Nigeria's ULAB recycling industry. In fact, most plants have never conducted any blood lead testing on workers or other stakeholders' (GIZ 2025 p.35). NESREA inspection (2025-11-28) found 'Absence of documented medical monitoring and regular blood-lead level testing for employees handling hazardous materials'. (GIZ 2025 ProBaMet Project Summary Report (S15), p.35, 2025-05-31; https://giz.de/sites/default/files/media/pkb-document/2025-09/giz2025-en-probamet.pdf)
  - Note: A7: recoded from 'public record of implementation found'; the sealing is recorded under enforcement_action. The basis is sector-wide: GIZ 2025 p.35 ('no such testing is conducted in Nigeria's ULAB recycling industry'). Ogun State announced free lead tests for residents and workers (allAfrica, 2025-12-01). Note: GIZ 2025 p.21 places blood-lead testing in Schedules 13-14; in the gazette it is in Reg. 48(1)(j), while Schedules 13 (B376) and 14 (B377) cover transport and recycling process controls.
- **D12, Schedules 13-14 (Reg. 35(1), 46(c)).**
  - Evidence: Violations include 'Improper battery breaking and acid management - Discharge of untreated lead-contaminated acid and effluents directly into the environment'. (NESREA press release, 'NESREA, Ogun State Govt. seal recycling facilities in Ogijo', 2025-11-28; https://nesrea.gov.ng/2025/11/28/nesrea-ogun-state-govt-seal-recycling-facilities-in-ogijo/)
  - Note: The record covers Ogijo facilities sealed on 2025-11-27/28. GIZ 2025 documents NESREA-led facility assessments (April and September 2024) and improvement plans (pp.14, 27). Scope (A7 follow-up): the evidence concerns the facilities sealed in Ogijo (NESREA, November 2025), not the sector as a whole.
- **D13, Reg. 7(2) (slag and residues).**
  - Evidence: 'Under the protocol, the facilities committed to manage ULAB slag and base metal residue in a safe, environmentally sound and socially responsible manner'; inspections reported slag sold 'to residents and truck drivers for landfilling and construction'. (NESREA press release, 'NESREA, Ogun State Govt. seal recycling facilities in Ogijo', 2025-11-28; https://nesrea.gov.ng/2025/11/28/nesrea-ogun-state-govt-seal-recycling-facilities-in-ogijo/)
  - Note: Regulation number for the slag provision is inferred from its position before Reg. 8 (PDF p.7). Scope (A7 follow-up): the evidence concerns the facilities sealed in Ogijo (NESREA, November 2025), not the sector as a whole.
- **D14, Reg. 43.**
  - Note: Cross-border inflows of used batteries are reported as illegal (Lead Battery Notes 2025; IDS 2016); this shows breaches, not a statement on implementation of the ban. No record of enforcement after 21 March 2024 was found.
- **D15, First Schedule item 3(x); Reg. 2(3).**
  - Evidence: GEM 2024 p.64: in Nigeria 'the EPR system is private sector-led, operationalized by the E-waste Producer [Responsibility Organisation]'. (Global E-waste Monitor 2024 (S34), p.64, 2024; https://ewastemonitor.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/GEM_2024_18-03_web_page_per_page_web.pdf)
  - Note: The record concerns the EPR system for EEE in general, not PV panels specifically.
- **D16, Reg. 3(3).**
  - Note: EPRON's 'registered members' and producer-registration pages returned HTTP 403/500 on 2026-11-05. Search summaries describe solar companies among listed members, but this was not verified. No record specific to PV panel importers was read.
- **D17, Reg. 12(1), (3)-(5), (7).**
  - Evidence: UNIDO, EPRON and E-Terra 'have begun discussions on a possible collaboration' on 'the responsible management of solar panels, inverters, batteries and other renewable-energy equipment at end-of-life'. (The Tide, 'Stakeholders partner to tackle renewable energy e-waste', 2026-09 (month of URL); https://www.thetidenewsonline.com/2026/09/stakeholders-partner-to-tackle-renewable-energy-e-waste/)
  - Note: No PV-specific take-back or collection record was found. An expert at a 2026-10-23 event said systems 'must be established to handle solar panels when they reach the end of their useful lives' (TheCable).
